Brain · Controller

Arduino UNO

Main Programmable Microcontroller

The programmable controller that acts as the brain of the robot, executing sensor logic and motor control loops.

ATmega328P 5V Logic 14 Digital Pins 16 MHz Clock

What is it?

An open-source microcontroller board based on the Microchip ATmega328P. It can be programmed via the Arduino IDE using C/C++ to read inputs and control external hardware.

Why do we use it?

It acts as the robot's central brain. It reads distance data from the three ultrasonic sensors, decides which path is clear, and sends driving commands to the motor driver shield.

Motor Control · Power

Motor Driver Shield

Cytron 2-Channel H-Bridge Driver

Controls the high-current power and rotational direction of the two DC motors safely from Arduino signals.

Dual H-Bridge PWM Speed (D9, D11) Direction (D8, D12) 12V Terminal

What is it?

An add-on expansion board that plugs directly onto the Arduino UNO header pins, containing high-current H-bridge driver circuits specifically designed for DC motors.

Why do we use it?

Arduino pins can only supply 5V at 20mA, which is not enough to spin drive motors. The shield safely draws 11–12V battery power and switches high current to both motors according to PWM speed and DIR logic.

Actuators · Propulsion

JGB37-520 DC Gear Motors

12V High-Torque Geared Drive Motors

Drive the robot forward, backward, and steer through differential speed control between left and right sides.

12V DC Rated 960 RPM Gearbox All-Metal Gears D-Type Shaft

What is it?

Direct-current electric motors integrated with heavy-duty metal reduction gearboxes. The gearbox trades high motor rotation speed for powerful driving torque.

Why do we use it?

Standard DC motors spin too fast with negligible torque. The 960 RPM metal gearbox provides the required pulling power to drive across tile and classroom floors without stalling.

Traction · Mobility

High-Traction Rubber Wheels

Direct-Fit Treaded Drive Wheels

Attach firmly to motor shafts and translate rotational torque into smooth forward drive and sharp turning.

High-Grip Rubber Anti-Slip Tread 4mm D-Hub Shock-Absorbing

What is it?

Rubber-tyred wheels with molded tread patterns designed to press-fit securely onto the 4mm D-shaped output shafts of the JGB37-520 gear motors.

Why do we use it?

Wheels convert motor rotation into linear motion. The rubber tread ensures strong friction and grip on smooth tile or wood, preventing wheel slipping during quick evasive turns.

Perception · Sensors

HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensors ×3

Acoustic Obstacle Detection Triplet

Measure precise distance to obstacles on the Left, Center, and Right using high-frequency ultrasonic sound waves.

40 kHz Ultrasonic 2 cm – 400 cm Range Left / Center / Right 3 mm Precision

What is it?

Acoustic distance measuring sensors with dual ultrasonic transducers (transmitter and receiver) that calculate distance by measuring sound travel time.

Why do we use it?

Positioned on the Left (TRIG=A0, ECHO=A1), Center (TRIG=A2, ECHO=A3), and Right (TRIG=A4, ECHO=A5), the three sensors form a forward radar allowing the robot to navigate and turn before collisions happen.

Frame · Chassis

2WD Robot Chassis Platform

Laser-Cut Acrylic Structural Base

The structural skeleton that securely holds motors, electronics, battery, and sensors into a single mobile robot.

2WD Platform Pre-Drilled Slots Front Caster Wheel Impact-Resistant

What is it?

A lightweight, durable laser-cut plastic/acrylic robotics platform equipped with pre-drilled mounting holes for brackets, hardware, and electronics.

Why do we use it?

Provides rigid mechanical support to mount the two drive motors, front caster wheel, Arduino board, motor shield, battery, and the three forward-facing ultrasonic sensors.

Power Supply · 12V

11–12V Rechargeable Battery

High-Capacity DC Power System

Provides the continuous electrical power required to drive both high-torque motors and onboard Arduino logic.

11.1V – 12V DC 3S Li-ion Cells High Discharge Regulated Supply

What is it?

A rechargeable lithium-ion battery pack supplying a stable 11.1V to 12V direct current output with heavy-gauge power leads and standard connector.

Why do we use it?

Standard 5V USB supplies cannot deliver the current required to drive two geared DC motors. The battery feeds the motor shield terminals directly, and the shield regulator powers the Arduino safely.

Wiring · Interconnect

Multi-Color Jumper Wires

DuPont Pin-to-Pin Interconnects

Connect the ultrasonic sensors and peripheral components to the Arduino UNO shield header pins without soldering.

Male-to-Female Male-to-Male Color-Coded Solderless

What is it?

Flexible insulated copper connecting wires terminated with molded DuPont female and male pins, designed for quick solderless breadboards and shield headers.

Why do we use it?

Routes 5V power, ground (GND), and signal lines (TRIG and ECHO) cleanly between the motor shield headers and all three ultrasonic sensors mounted on the front chassis.

How the Robot Works

The robot continuously reads its three ultrasonic sensors and uses that information to steer itself away from obstacles.

Ultrasonic Sensors
↓
Arduino UNO
↓
Decision Logic
↓
Motor Driver Shield
↓
Left + Right Motors
↓
Robot Movement

Sensor Positions

LEFTTRIG D2 / ECHO D3
CENTERTRIG D4 / ECHO D5
RIGHTTRIG D6 / ECHO D7

The center sensor detects obstacles directly ahead. The left and right sensors detect obstacles on either side.

Decision Rules (from the code)

  • Center < 20 cm → Back up briefly, then turn left
  • Left < 10 cm → Turn right in place
  • Right < 10 cm → Turn left in place
  • Clear path → Move forward at full speed

🔋 Power Connection

• The 11–12V battery connects to the motor driver shield's main power input terminals.

• The motor driver shield provides regulated power to the Arduino UNO via the shield's pin connection.

• The ultrasonic sensors use 5V and GND from the Arduino or shield headers.

⚠ Do not connect 11–12V directly to Arduino signal or I/O pins.

Required Libraries

✓ No External Libraries Required

The Arduino program uses only standard, built-in Arduino functions. No additional library installation is needed in the Arduino IDE.

Functions used in this program:

  • digitalWrite() — sets a pin HIGH or LOW
  • analogWrite() — generates PWM signal to control motor speed
  • pulseIn() — measures the echo pulse duration from ultrasonic sensors
  • millis() — non-blocking time tracking for sensor intervals
  • delay() and delayMicroseconds() — timing for trigger pulses
  • Serial.begin() / Serial.print() — serial output for debugging sensor values

Arduino Program

Upload this program to the Arduino UNO using the Arduino IDE.

arduino_code.ino 
﻿/*
   Obstacle Avoidance Robot - Arduino Uno
   - 3 Ultrasonic sensors (Left, Center, Right)
   - All motor movement controlled via motorControl(leftSpeed, rightSpeed)
*/

// ==========================================
// PIN DEFINITIONS
// ==========================================

// Motor Driver Pins (PWM pins on Uno: 3,5,6,9,10,11)
const int PWM1 = 9;   // Left motor speed
const int DIR1 = 8;   // Left motor direction
const int PWM2 = 11;  // Right motor speed
const int DIR2 = 12;  // Right motor direction (D12)

// Ultrasonic Sensor Pins
const int trig1 = 2;  const int echo1 = 3;   // Left sensor
const int trig2 = 4;  const int echo2 = 5;   // Center sensor
const int trig3 = 6;  const int echo3 = 7;   // Right sensor

// ==========================================
// SETTINGS
// ==========================================

const float MAX_SENSOR_DISTANCE       = 200.0;  // cm
const float FRONT_OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD  = 20.0;   // cm - center sensor triggers backup + turn
const float SIDE_OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD   = 10.0;   // cm - left/right sensors trigger turn
const unsigned long SENSOR_INTERVAL   = 50;     // ms between sensor reads
const unsigned long BACKUP_DURATION   = 50;    // ms to reverse when front obstacle detected

unsigned long lastSensorReadTime = 0;

// ==========================================
// MOTOR CONTROL
// ==========================================

void motor1(int speed) {
  if (speed > 0) {
    digitalWrite(DIR1, HIGH);
    analogWrite(PWM1, speed);       // Forward
  } else if (speed < 0) {
    digitalWrite(DIR1, LOW);
    analogWrite(PWM1, -speed);      // Backward (negate to get positive PWM value)
  } else {
    digitalWrite(DIR1, LOW);
    analogWrite(PWM1, 0);           // Stop
  }
}

void motor2(int speed) {
  if (speed > 0) {
    digitalWrite(DIR2, HIGH);
    analogWrite(PWM2, speed);       // Forward
  } else if (speed < 0) {
    digitalWrite(DIR2, LOW);
    analogWrite(PWM2, -speed);      // Backward (negate to get positive PWM value)
  } else {
    digitalWrite(DIR2, LOW);
    analogWrite(PWM2, 0);           // Stop
  }
}

void motorControl(int leftSpeed, int rightSpeed) {
  motor1(leftSpeed);
  motor2(rightSpeed);
}

// ==========================================
// SETUP
// ==========================================

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(9600);

  // Ultrasonic pins
  pinMode(trig1, OUTPUT); pinMode(echo1, INPUT);
  pinMode(trig2, OUTPUT); pinMode(echo2, INPUT);
  pinMode(trig3, OUTPUT); pinMode(echo3, INPUT);

  // Motor pins
  pinMode(PWM1, OUTPUT); pinMode(DIR1, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(PWM2, OUTPUT); pinMode(DIR2, OUTPUT);

  motorControl(0, 0); // Start stopped
  Serial.println("Robot Ready!");
}

// ==========================================
// MAIN LOOP
// ==========================================

void loop() {
  unsigned long currentMillis = millis();


  if (currentMillis - lastSensorReadTime >= SENSOR_INTERVAL) {
    lastSensorReadTime = currentMillis;

    float leftDist   = getDistance(trig1, echo1);
    float centerDist = getDistance(trig2, echo2);
    float rightDist  = getDistance(trig3, echo3);

    Serial.print(leftDist);
    Serial.print("       ");
    Serial.print(centerDist);
    Serial.print("       ");
    Serial.println(rightDist);


    if (centerDist < FRONT_OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD) {
      // Back up briefly, then turn left for the same duration
      motorControl(-255, -255);
      delay(BACKUP_DURATION);
      motorControl(-255, 255);  // Turn Left in place
      delay(BACKUP_DURATION);
    }
    else if (leftDist < SIDE_OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD) {
      motorControl(255, -255);  // Turn Right in place
    }
    else if (rightDist < SIDE_OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD) {
      motorControl(-255, 255);  // Turn Left in place
    }
    else {
      motorControl(255, 255);   // Go Forward
    }
  }
}

// ==========================================
// ULTRASONIC DISTANCE FUNCTION
// ==========================================

float getDistance(int trigPin, int echoPin) {
  digitalWrite(trigPin, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(2);
  digitalWrite(trigPin, HIGH);
  delayMicroseconds(10);
  digitalWrite(trigPin, LOW);

  long timeout = (MAX_SENSOR_DISTANCE * 2.0 / 0.0343) + 3000;
  long duration = pulseIn(echoPin, HIGH, timeout);

  if (duration == 0) return MAX_SENSOR_DISTANCE;

  float distance = duration * 0.0343 / 2.0;

  if (distance > MAX_SENSOR_DISTANCE) return MAX_SENSOR_DISTANCE;
  if (distance < 0) return 0.0;

  return distance;

}

Assembly & Setup

Follow these steps to build the Autonomous Racer Robot.

  1. Prepare the Chassis

    Lay out the plastic chassis and identify the mounting points for motors, sensors, and electronics before starting assembly.

  2. Attach Motors and Wheels

    Mount the two JGB37-520 motors onto the chassis brackets. Attach one rubber wheel firmly to each motor shaft.

  3. Mount Arduino UNO and Motor Driver Shield

    Press the motor driver shield onto the Arduino UNO header pins. Mount this assembly securely onto the chassis.

  4. Mount the Three Ultrasonic Sensors

    Position one sensor at the Left, one at the Center, and one at the Right of the robot front. Secure them facing horizontally forward.

  5. Connect Sensor Wires

    Using jumper wires, connect each sensor: VCC→5V, GND→GND, and TRIG/ECHO to the Arduino pins shown in the Connections section.

  6. Connect Motor Wires

    Connect the left motor to shield Motor 1 terminals (PWM=D9, DIR=D8) and the right motor to Motor 2 terminals (PWM=D11, DIR=D12). Verify terminal labels on your specific shield.

  7. Connect the Power Supply

    Connect the 11–12V battery to the motor driver shield's power input. Check polarity carefully before connecting.

  8. Upload the Arduino Program

    Connect the Arduino UNO to a computer via USB and upload the program. See the Code section for step-by-step instructions.

  9. Test the Robot

    Place the robot on a flat, clear floor. Power it on and verify it moves forward and steers around obstacles. Use the Serial Monitor (9600 baud) to view live sensor distances if needed.

Safety Notes

⚠️Check battery polarity before connecting it to the circuit. Reversed polarity can permanently damage components.
⚠️Do not short-circuit the battery terminals. Never let bare wires touch both terminals at the same time.
⚠️Do not connect the 11–12V battery directly to Arduino signal or I/O pins. Use the motor driver shield's regulated supply path.
⚠️Keep fingers, loose wires, and clothing away from rotating wheels and motors while the robot is powered on.
⚠️Test the robot on a clear, flat surface away from ledges, stairs, and obstacles that could cause it to fall.
